# Proposal to IEEE Robotics and Automation Magazine — survey feature (draft 11 Nov 2026, for Paul and Ken)

**Proposed title:** Robotics in Africa: A Survey of Industry, Talent, Research and Policy

**Proposed authors:** Kenechukwu C. Mbanisi (African Robotics Network; Olin College of Engineering), Paul Amayo (African
Robotics Unit, University of Cape Town), and contributors to be agreed. *[Order and the contributor list to be settled with Ken.]*

**Type:** survey feature, ~4,500 words, nine magazine pages, ten figures.

## Summary

Robotics is often discussed in Africa either as a leapfrogging technology or as one ill-suited to economies with abundant
labour, and the evidence that could settle the question has been fragmented and largely unreported. The proposed article
surveys the state of robotics across the continent through four connected pillars, namely industry, talent and workforce,
research and innovation, and policy and regulation. It draws on the first continental study of the field, *The State of
Robotics in Africa 2026* (African Robotics Network, June 2026, doi 10.5281/zenodo.20749780), a mixed-methods report built
from desk research, around eighty documented industry use cases and fifteen interviews with startup founders, educators,
university leaders, industry experts and African Union officials in seven countries. The report has not been peer reviewed,
and the article is its condensed, peer-reviewed form for the robotics community, with the evidence presented as figures
redrawn from the report's data.

The central observation of the survey is that robotics in Africa is adopted to fill gaps in productivity, data, safety and
access rather than to substitute labour. Aerial robots account for 41.8 % of the documented use cases, and agriculture and
environmental monitoring lead adoption, while the continent's robot density in manufacturing remains around ten per
10,000 workers. The people, research and policy that should sustain this adoption exist but are fragmented: around sixty
universities offer robotics-related programmes, mostly at undergraduate level, and around sixty laboratories conduct
robotics research, yet over 2014–2025 authors with African affiliations contributed 71 papers to ICRA and IROS, concentrated
in three institutions, with about two thirds written with collaborators outside the continent and a single paper
co-authored across two African countries.

## Timeliness and relevance to RA-M readers

The IEEE Robotics and Automation Society has invested in this community over the last three years, through the Robotics in
Africa forums at ICAR 2023, IROS 2024 and ICRA 2025, ICRA@40 Africa in Johannesburg in 2024 and the Robotics and Automation
in Africa workshop at the Deep Learning Indaba. A survey that places these initiatives within the wider ecosystem gives the
Society's members an evidence base for where collaboration, research agendas and venues can have the most effect. The
article closes with recommendations directed at the robotics community itself: regional research collaboration, field and
aerial robotics agendas matched to the adoption pattern, teleoperation services, low-cost kits and competitions, and hosting
events on the continent.

## Outline

1. Introduction: why robotics, why now, and the gap-filling observation.
2. How the evidence was gathered, and its limits.
3. Where robots meet the field: platforms, sectors, regional signatures and the actors behind adoption.
4. The people: programmes, competitions, and the educator and vocational gaps.
5. The knowledge base: laboratories, output at the major venues, collaboration patterns and community initiatives.
6. Policy and regulation: AI frameworks, drones as the entry point to regulation, and lessons from emerging adopters.
7. What the robotics community can do.
