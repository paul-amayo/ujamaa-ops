# Section 4.8 (MuST-C): how it can honestly be framed, given the verdicts as registered

A reading for Paul from JS-2, 12 Nov 2026. No manuscript edits. Numbers are the as-registered records of 11 Nov (plot 198
unless named); T4 rests on provisional premarks. The sensitivity table shows that none of items 30 to 33 turns a failed test
into a pass on plot 198, so the choice below does not wait on those decisions; at most two or three sentences of framing A
would change wording.

## What the registered verdicts say

The rig stops fit (T1, 0.43-0.58 px on 18 of 18) and 14 of 18 register to the UAV1 reference (T2; the four 230628 stops wait
for a hand initialisation and a rule for how such a stop is checked). Measured the way Section 3.4 prescribes, the trial's
own same-day floor says that per-plant association is not resolvable at its 0.19 m in-row spacing, for the laser (9.7 cm)
as well as for the splat (12.1 cm at 0.15 m, 16.2 cm at 0.20 m), against a cap of half the spacing (9.5 cm); the growth
threshold is measured at 3.2 cm (T3). The splat census misses seedlings: on 1 June its recall is 0.68 of the laser's (T4,
provisional; 0.905 on 13 June, a pass by 0.005). The 2 June removal shows at the level of the sampling polygon: inside it the
laser's canopy cover stays at 3.1 % on 13 June while the control rises from 4.0 to 29.4 %, and the laser calls 8 of its 10
plants removed (and 3 of 12 in the control polygon); the splat records only 5 of those 10 and calls 4 removed (2 of 9 in the
control), so the per-plant removal test fails by detection,
not by the change rule (T5). The splat's growth agrees with the laser's within g over 1 to 13 June (12 plants, median
difference 1.3 cm, 12 of 12 calls agree; flagged because 1 June's own same-day threshold exceeds 8 cm) and over 1 to 21
June with the two-day bound subtracted (10 plants, 9 of 10), and fails over 13 to 21 June (9 plants, 7 of 9 calls) (T6).
The splat reads beet tops 3 to 9 cm below the laser, as every UAV sensor does, and fails the plant clause on all 12 stops
(T7). The claim as the design wrote it, that the ledger calls the destructively removed plants and no others per plant,
does not hold on this trial.

## Framing A: a limits subsection, kept in full

Section 4.8 stays, with Table 7 and Fig 8, and reports what holds at polygon level and what does not per plant. It claims
three things: that the method's rule of measuring the thresholds from same-day data transfers and does its work, since on
a drilled crop it declares per-plant association unresolvable for both sensors instead of producing a found-again rate;
that the destructive removal is seen at the level of the sampling polygon by both sensors; and that the growth the splat
reads agrees with the laser within the measured threshold over one 12-day interval and the 20-day span. It concedes that
the per-plant change claims do not hold, that the splat census is detection-limited on seedlings, that the splat's heights
are biased low by the same amount as the UAV photogrammetry, and that the trial exercises the ledger's rules but not the
pose graph (there is no robot trajectory). It is the longest option and the one most exposed to the open items: its
detection sentence waits for the hand marks, its T7 sentence reads differently under item 31 (4 of 12 stops within the UAV
floor, with the bias as the finding), and the second site's association sentence reads differently under item 33.

## Framing B: a short paragraph, the evidence in the supplement

Two or three sentences, in Section 4.8 or in the limits paragraph of the Discussion, with Table 7 and Fig 8 moved to the
supplementary material. It claims only what is final regardless of the marks and of items 30 to 33: with the thresholds
measured the same way, association at this plant spacing is not resolvable for either sensor; the removal is seen at region
level; growth agrees with the laser over one interval. It must say in the same sentences that per-plant detection, removal
and height fail, or a reader will take the omission for concealment. It concedes the per-plant results to the supplement and
makes no claim that rests on T4.

## Framing C: removal

Section 4.8, Table 7, Fig 8 and the MuST-C lines of the data availability and acknowledgements come out, and the paper rests
on the nursery. It claims nothing about a second crop and concedes nothing in the paper. What it gives up is the only
external truth in the paper (a laser and destructive sampling), and the experiment was designed for this paper, with its
heading already in the draft; taken out after the results, it reads as selective reporting unless the reason given is one
of scope, that the paper's subject is the pose graph, which MuST-C cannot exercise, and the full negative result is reported
elsewhere.
