# One Question

Google.org Food Security Collaborative progress video, UJAMAA film. Final script for recording, 2 November 2026. Runtime 2:44.5 (164.5 s). No music; natural silence under the narration. Narration recorded by Paul, first person plural; the two 2023 lines are his archive audio from the EBE talk (22 May 2023), marked on screen as 2023, picture never shown.

## The unique claim

UJAMAA's 3D farm model carries identity inside the model itself, so that every point is labelled with the plant and the row it belongs to; this is what lets a question asked in any of eight languages light exactly one tree, lets the same bags be matched again four months later, and lets the pipeline built for a camera-and-LiDAR robot run from an ordinary phone walk on a Kenyan smallholding.

## The arc

1. The question (0-18 s): a Kiswahili question is typed into the live app, the assistant starts reading the farm record, and we say who we are: UJAMAA at the University of Cape Town, built to answer it.

2. The record (18-55 s): that takes a system which knows the farm as the farmer does, down to the row, the tree and what is growing on it; the farm's record begins with a robot survey (2023 citrus lane; 2025 and 2026 tunnels; robot shot pending clearance, substituted); the 3D model carries not only colour but also meaning: one row lights, every tree, then exactly one tree.

3. The memory (55-70 s): once a plant has a name it can be found again; Klapmuts tunnels surveyed four months apart, 825 of 912 bags matched from one survey to the next, shown as the registry map taking its row colours.

4. The answer (70-92 s): the same name lets a question reach the plant; the Kiswahili answer lands as the timer ends, tree five with 61 oranges, the render circles them; eight languages, on our own machine; when the record holds no answer it says so.

5. The turn (92-121 s): all of this needed a robot, and in May 2023, before the first survey, we said this: Paul's 2023 voice on who can afford such data, then on lightweight techniques for mobile phones, over the robot-built orchard and the globe travelling to Kenya; the picture cuts on the word phones.

6. The reveal (121-164.5 s): May 2026, two Kenyan smallholdings, an ordinary phone, without a robot or LiDAR; the same pipeline built the farm models from the phone alone (29 cabbages in two rows, each with a name; the ripe chillies lit; a ground crop from one short walk); the same kind of question lights one cabbage; robotics opened the path, the phone walks it; end card in silence over the walk.
